# The Stories I Tell Arlo

Today is Alicia's birthday.

It's the second one since she's been gone, and for whatever reason, this one is hitting me pretty
hard.

Grief is strange like that. There are dates you know are going to hurt. Birthdays, anniversaries,
holidays. You can see them coming from a mile away and convince yourself you're prepared for them.
Then sometimes it isn't even the date. It's a dog, a picture, a place you haven't thought about in
years, or some random memory that suddenly feels like it happened yesterday.

Lately I've been thinking a lot about some of the first trips Alicia and I took together.

I don't remember all the details anymore. That's probably one of the things bothering me as I write
this. There are pieces that are incredibly clear and others that have faded together over the years.

But I remember her. And I remember us.

## New Orleans

One of our first trips together was to New Orleans.

I had never been. Alicia planned it, of course.

She found us a nice little hotel in the French Quarter and somehow we scraped enough money together
to make the trip. We drove my dad's Mercedes down there and then, once we arrived, mostly relied on
public transportation and our feet because I think we had spent most of our money just getting
there.

We didn't need much anyway.

We walked around. A lot.

We visited cemeteries, wandered through the French Quarter, walked along the river, and watched
street musicians and random groups of people gathering and making music. We also saw someone get
stabbed, because apparently that was part of the authentic New Orleans experience Alicia had booked
for us.

I don't remember what restaurants we ate at. I don't remember the name of the hotel. I couldn't tell
you what bus or streetcar we rode. But I remember walking around with her.

That's what has stuck with me.

We didn't have a bunch of money. There wasn't some perfectly planned itinerary filled with expensive
experiences. We were just somewhere neither of us was normally supposed to be, wandering around
together. And somehow that was enough.

It usually was.

## We Should Go to the Beach

Another memory that has been floating around in my head lately is Virginia Beach.

Alicia wanted to go to the beach. So we went.

There wasn't months of planning or some big vacation scheduled on the calendar. As I remember it, it
was basically, "Let's go to the beach," followed by getting into her car and driving to Virginia.

I'm not even a beach person. But I wanted to be wherever she was.

There was more going on in her life at the time than just wanting to see the ocean. Her dad had
passed away. She had recently gotten out of a long-term relationship. And Alicia and I were still
figuring out exactly what the hell we were to each other.

Looking back now, I think she was running away from some pain for a couple days. Maybe I knew that
then too. I just went with her.

I think we left in the middle of the night. We drove until we got there and slept for a little while
in the back of her car waiting for the sun to come up. Then we went down to the beach and watched
the sunrise over the ocean.

It wasn't even really beach weather. I think it was fall, although I honestly don't remember
anymore. I know it wasn't warm enough to swim. We were only there for a weekend and probably spent
almost as much time driving as we did actually being in Virginia Beach.

But I remember walking along the shops and the pier.

For some reason I remember spending time around the dog portion of the beach, even though we didn't
have our dogs with us. We may not have even had Coco and Annie yet. Maybe we were already the people
who would eventually become obsessed with our dogs and just didn't know it yet.

On the way home we deliberately took a different route so we could drive across that enormous bridge
over the water and come back through the Washington, D.C. area.

Again, the details are fuzzy. But I remember being happy. I remember being tired. I remember
driving. I remember the sunrise.

And I remember Alicia sitting beside me.

## And Now There's Arlo

Maybe that's why I've been thinking about those trips so much lately. There is someone new riding
around with me now.

Arlo.

Alicia would have absolutely loved him.

I know people say things like that after someone dies. We imagine what they would think about the
things they never got to see. But there isn't much imagination required with this one.

She would have loved that little asshole.

She would have loved how smart he is. She would have laughed when he was being a shithead. She would
have spoiled him more than I wanted her to and then denied doing it. And I would have complained
about it while secretly loving watching the two of them together.

That's one of the things that hurts most about having him.

Coco and Annie have Alicia in them.

Not literally, obviously. But she helped raise them. She loved them, talked to them, corrected them,
spoiled them, and took them places. She helped turn them into the dogs they became. Part of who they
are was shaped by her.

Arlo won't have that.

And I hate that.

So I tell him about her. Yes, I realize he's a dog. I don't care.

I tell him how amazing she was. I tell him that there was someone who should have been here to meet
him. Someone who would have loved him as much as I do.

I wish she could have left some of herself on him the way she did Coco and Annie. Since she can't, I
suppose it's my job to try.

Maybe some of that happens through me. Maybe when I take him somewhere just because it sounds fun,
that's her. Maybe when we get in the car without much of a plan, that's her. Maybe when I let him
stop and explore instead of worrying about where we're supposed to be going, that's her.

Maybe someday I'll take him to the ocean.

## The Parts That Remain

One of the scary things about losing someone is realizing that memories fade.

I hate admitting that.

I wish I could perfectly remember every conversation, every trip, every stupid joke, every little
hotel we stayed in, every restaurant, every road.

I can't.

There are already details from New Orleans and Virginia Beach that I can't pull back no matter how
hard I try. And these trips weren't even that long ago. It's strange to think that something that
meant so much to me can already have pieces missing.

But maybe remembering everything isn't the point.

I remember driving my dad's Mercedes to New Orleans when we barely had enough money to be there. I
remember wandering around the French Quarter, the cemeteries, and the street musicians.

I remember sleeping in the back of Alicia's car because she wanted to see the ocean. I remember
standing beside her watching the sun come up. I remember choosing the long way home just because
there was something interesting to see.

Mostly, I remember that when Alicia said, "Let's go," I went.

And I'm so fucking thankful that I did.

Because all these years later, on her birthday, those ridiculous little trips are some of the things
I treasure most.

Today she should be here.

She should be laughing at Arlo, telling me I'm being too strict with him, and loving on Coco and
Annie. She should be planning another trip somewhere and telling me about all the things we were
going to do while I pretended I wasn't interested.

Instead, I have the stories. I have Coco and Annie. I have Arlo. And I have a lifetime of little
pieces of her scattered throughout everything I do.

So I'll keep telling him about her.

I'll tell him about the woman who would have loved him. I'll tell him about New Orleans. I'll tell
him about sleeping in the back of a car at Virginia Beach waiting for the sun to come up.

I'll tell him that some of the best moments of my life started because Alicia wanted to go somewhere
and I was lucky enough to go with her.

Happy birthday, Alicia.

I miss you. I love you.

And I'm still telling your stories.
