# The Quiet Things That No One Ever Knows

It has been almost a year and a half since Alicia died.

That's a strange sentence to write because sometimes it feels like it has been forever, and
sometimes it feels like it just happened.

Lately I've been looking through a lot of old pictures. Maybe it's because we're coming into fall.
There was always something about this time of year that fit Alicia. Hoodie weather, Halloween,
camping, cooking for the holidays. Although, if I'm being fair, she probably enjoyed eating the food
at least as much as cooking it.

This was also around the time of year when her and her lady friends would usually take a girls trip.

There are certain seasons that seem to carry people with them. Fall carries Alicia for me.

## The Quiet Things

I've found a lot of comfort in routines since she died. They're probably incredibly boring from the
outside.

I make my bed every morning now. I walk the dogs at least four times a day. I clean the house on the
weekends. Once a month we have what I call "spa weekend," where Coco and Annie get baths, their
nails done, flea medicine, and whatever other maintenance I've decided they need.

I've also been cooking for myself. Alicia was a great cook and an even better host. She loved having
friends over, feeding everybody, and making sure people were taken care of. I can't help but wonder
sometimes if she would be proud of me standing in the kitchen actually cooking meals for myself now.
Probably while also telling me I was doing something wrong :).

I read somewhere that smiling can actually make you happier, so I've started intentionally smiling
sometimes while I'm walking the dogs. I have no idea if it works. There's a pretty good chance I
just look like an idiot walking around the neighborhood smiling at nothing. But I do it anyway.

These aren't exactly major accomplishments. Nobody sees you make your bed in the morning. Nobody
congratulates you because the house is clean on Sunday night. Nobody knows that getting the dogs
walked four times today was part of keeping yourself moving forward.

They're just the quiet things. And maybe that's what a lot of grief eventually becomes.

## It's Not You, It's Me

I've kept to myself quite a bit. I know that probably looks strange to some people. Maybe even
concerning sometimes. But I've realized that I genuinely find comfort in the life I've built around
these little routines.

The dogs need walked. The house needs cleaned. Dinner needs made. Work needs done. Tomorrow morning
the bed gets made again. There is something reassuring about knowing what comes next.

I don't think I'm hiding from the world. At least not entirely. I think I've just become protective
of the peace I've managed to create.

It's not that I don't care about people.

It's not you, it's me!

And right now, quiet is pretty comfortable.

## Harvest Moon

This past weekend I went camping at our friends' house for the harvest moon.

It was one of those weekends that was both really good and really hard without being obviously
either one. There were kids everywhere. I kept thinking about how great Alicia would have been with
them.

People kept talking about how good Coco and Annie looked and how well they were doing around
everybody. They were social, happy, wandering around like they owned the place, waiting for any
dropped food.

I was proud of them. And then I thought about how proud Alicia would have been of them.

Coco is about to turn eleven.

That one hurts a little more than I like to admit.

Alicia loved those dogs. They were part of virtually every version of our life together. Camping
trips, hikes, weekends at the lake, laying around the house doing absolutely nothing. In so many of
my memories, if Alicia is there, the dogs aren't very far away. That love of the outdoors, camping
and the dogs was always part of who she was.

Sometimes I'm proud of myself for how well I've taken care of them. Sometimes I wish she could see
it. Most of the time it's both.

## Before and After

Looking through pictures has been harder than I expected.

There isn't some obvious dividing line where our old life ends.

You just keep scrolling.

There's Alicia looking completely normal.

There's a camping trip.

There's dinner.

There's the dogs.

There's some stupid picture that probably made sense at the time.

And then suddenly she's wearing a hospital gown.

And somehow she still looks good.

Then there are more hospital pictures.

More appointments.

And three months later she's gone.

It's almost impossible for my brain to reconcile the speed of it when I see everything laid out
chronologically like that.

One of the pictures that gets me the most isn't even from the hospital. It's her passport photo. We
were supposed to go to Alaska with my family. When that picture was taken, we were still planning a
trip. There was still an Alaska in our future.

She never got to go and I couldn't bring myself to go without her.

There is something incredibly cruel about being able to scroll backward through your phone and watch
a future disappear.

At the time, none of those pictures knew what was coming.

We didn't either.

## Fall

Maybe that's why fall has been hitting me harder.

She should be here.

She should be planning Halloween.

She should be talking about some girls trip.

She should be cooking way too much food for Christmas and then pretending the eating wasn't actually
her favorite part.

She should have been sitting around that campground this weekend.

She should get to see Coco turn eleven.

I know that's not how any of this works.

I've had almost a year and a half to learn that.

But grief doesn't seem particularly interested in what you know.

## Still Here

I used to wonder constantly whether any of this would ever get easier. I don't know if easier is the
right word. Different might be.

I can look back now and recognize that I've built something resembling a life again. It isn't the
life I expected. There are pieces missing from it that aren't coming back.

But there is dinner cooking in the kitchen.

There are two dogs waiting impatiently for another walk.

There is a clean house.

There is a bed that I'll probably make again tomorrow morning.

There are friends around a campfire beneath a harvest moon.

And sometimes there's some idiot walking down the street smiling to himself because he read
somewhere that it might help.

I don't know whether Alicia would be proud of all of that.

I hope she would be.

I think she would probably make fun of me for some of it.

Actually, I know she would.

And strangely enough, that thought makes me smile too.
